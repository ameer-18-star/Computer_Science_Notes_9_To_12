## 8.1 Secure Computer Usage

### 🎬 Story Mode: The Unlocked Door

Imagine you live in a house. Every night before sleeping, you lock the front door. You would never go to bed leaving the door wide open, because anyone could walk in.

Your computer is like a house. Your files, photos, passwords, and personal information live inside. **If you don't "lock" your computer properly, strangers can walk in and take everything.**

Secure computer usage means locking every digital door — so your information stays yours.

---

### What Does "Secure Computer Usage" Mean?

**Secure computer usage** means using your computer in a way that protects you from harm. This includes:

- Protecting your personal information (name, address, phone number, passwords).
- Making smart choices about which hardware and software to use.
- Making sure your online activities do not hurt you or anyone else.

Let's break this down into three parts.

---

### Part 1: Choosing the Right Hardware and Software

**Hardware** = the physical parts of a computer. Examples: keyboard, monitor, CPU, mouse.

**Software** = the programs you run on a computer. Examples: Microsoft Word, a game, an antivirus program.

Choosing the right hardware and software matters for three reasons:

#### ✅ Safety
Outdated software has weaknesses. Hackers know how to attack old software. Always use updated, trusted software. For example, using a computer without updated **antivirus software** is like leaving your door open. Antivirus software is a program that finds and removes harmful programs (called **viruses** or **malware**) from your computer.

#### ✅ Efficiency
The right hardware makes your work smooth and fast. Imagine trying to run a modern video game on a computer that is 15 years old — it won't work. Good hardware means your tasks are completed quickly and easily.

#### ✅ Compatibility
**Compatibility** means that your hardware and software are designed to work together. Before buying or installing new software, always check the **system requirements** — the minimum hardware your computer needs to run the software properly. For example, if a game requires 8 GB of RAM and your computer only has 4 GB, the game will not run.

> **Visual Tip — Draw This!**
> Draw a triangle. Label the three corners: **Safety**, **Efficiency**, **Compatibility**. In the middle, write: **Good Computer Usage**. This triangle shows that all three must be balanced.

---

### Part 2: Protecting Your Personal Information

**Personal information** is any data that can identify you. Examples:
- Your full name
- Your home address
- Your phone number
- Your date of birth
- Your passwords

**Why protect it?** Because if the wrong person gets your personal information, they can steal your identity, access your accounts, or even harm you physically.

**Practical Walkthrough: How to Protect Your Personal Information**

1. **Never share your passwords** with anyone — not even your best friend.
2. **Log out of accounts** after using a shared computer (school lab, library).
3. **Do not fill out forms** on websites unless you are 100% sure the website is trusted.
4. **Check for HTTPS** in the web address. HTTPS means the website is secure. HTTP (without the "S") is not secure. The "S" stands for **Secure**.
5. **Think before you type.** Ask yourself: "Would I be comfortable if everyone could see this?" If not, don't type it online.

> 📌 **Quick Recap:** Secure computer usage means choosing safe hardware and software, keeping your personal data private, and always being aware of the risks around you.

## 8.2 Responsible Computer Usage

### 🎬 Story Mode: Ahmed and the Fake Email

Ahmed received an email. It said:

*"Dear Ahmed, your bank account has been locked. Click here immediately to unlock it. Enter your full name, date of birth, and account password."*

Ahmed was nervous. He clicked the link. The website looked real. He entered his information.

Three days later, his family's bank account was emptied.

What happened? Ahmed was the victim of a **phishing scam** — a fake message designed to trick you into giving away personal information.

**The lesson?** Responsible computer usage means knowing how tricks like this work — and never falling for them.

---

### What Is Responsible Computer Usage?

Being responsible means making **thoughtful, careful decisions** every time you use a computer. This includes:

- Using secure digital platforms
- Creating strong passwords
- Protecting your accounts with extra layers of security
- Recognizing and avoiding scams
- Backing up your important data

---

### Use of Secure Digital Platforms

A **digital platform** is any online service you use — social media, email, online banking, school portals, gaming accounts. Using these platforms **securely** requires six key habits.

---

#### Habit 1: Using Strong Passwords 🔐

A **password** is the key to your digital house. A weak password is a broken key — anyone can copy it.

**What makes a password strong?**

A strong password has ALL of the following:
- At least **8 characters** (longer is better — aim for 12+)
- A mix of **uppercase and lowercase letters** (A, b, C)
- At least one **number** (3, 7, 9)
- At least one **special character** (!@#$%^&*)

**Example:**

| Weak Password | Strong Password |
|---|---|
| `ahmed123` | `Ahn3d!sMy$T@r` |
| `password` | `P@$$w0rd#2024` |
| `sara` | `S@r@L0v3sC0d3!` |

> **Visual Tip — The Password Strength Gauge:**
> Imagine a fuel gauge in a car. On the far left it says **EMPTY (Weak)** and on the far right it says **FULL (Strong)**. The needle moves right as you add length, numbers, uppercase letters, and special characters. A password like `ahmed` sits near EMPTY. A password like `Ahn3d!sMy$T@r` sits near FULL.

**Practical Walkthrough: Creating a Strong Password**

1. Think of a short sentence you will remember. Example: *"I love Computer Science in 2024!"*
2. Take the first letter of each word: `ILCS2024`
3. Replace some letters with numbers or symbols: `!LCS#2024`
4. Add an uppercase and a special character at the end: `!LCS#2024@`
5. **Result:** `!LCS#2024@` — This is a strong, memorable password.

> 💡 **Never use:** your name, birthday, phone number, or the word "password" as your password.

---

#### Habit 2: Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) 🛡️

Even a strong password can sometimes be stolen. **Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)** adds a second lock to your account.

**2FA means:** After you enter your password, the system asks you to prove your identity a **second time** — usually by sending a code to your phone number.

Think of it like this: Your school locker has a combination lock AND a key. Even if someone knows the combination, they still need the key to open it. **2FA is the second key.**

**How 2FA Works — Step by Step:**

1. You go to a website (e.g., your email).
2. You type your **username** and **password**.
3. The system sends a **6-digit code** to your registered phone number via SMS.
4. You enter the code on the website.
5. Only then are you allowed inside.

**Why is this powerful?** Even if a hacker steals your password, they cannot log in without your phone. They have your key, but not your lock.

> 💡 **Always enable 2FA** on important accounts: email, school portal, social media.

---

#### Habit 3: Avoiding Public Wi-Fi for Sensitive Transactions 📶

**Public Wi-Fi** is internet available for everyone in a public place — like a café, airport, or library. It sounds convenient. But it is dangerous for sensitive activities.

**Why?** Public Wi-Fi is often **not encrypted**. Encryption means your data is scrambled so others cannot read it. On a public Wi-Fi network without encryption, other people on the same network can sometimes **see what you are typing** — including passwords and bank details.

**The Rule:** Never do these things on public Wi-Fi:
- Online banking
- Entering passwords
- Shopping with your credit/debit card
- Accessing medical or personal records

**What IS safe on public Wi-Fi?**
- Reading news
- Watching YouTube videos
- General browsing (not entering any personal data)

> **Visual Tip — The Danger Zone:**
> Draw a circle. Inside it, write "HOME Wi-Fi = SAFE." Outside the circle, write "PUBLIC Wi-Fi = DANGER ZONE for sensitive tasks." This shows the boundary clearly.

---

#### Habit 4: Being Aware of Scams and Phishing 🎣

**Phishing** is when a criminal sends a fake message pretending to be a real company — to trick you into giving your personal information.

The word "phishing" sounds like "fishing." The criminal is **fishing for your information** using a fake bait (the fake message).

**How to Spot a Phishing Message:**

| Warning Sign | Example |
|---|---|
| Urgent, scary language | "Your account will be deleted in 24 hours!" |
| Asks for your password | "Enter your password to verify your account" |
| Sender email looks fake | `support@bank-secure-login.com` instead of `support@bank.com` |
| Links go to strange websites | Hovering over a link shows an unrelated URL |
| Grammar and spelling errors | "Pleese click hear to verify you're acount" |

**Practical Walkthrough: What to Do When You Receive a Suspicious Message**

1. **Do NOT click** any links in the message.
2. **Do NOT reply** to the message.
3. **Check the sender's email address** carefully. Is it the real company's address?
4. **Go directly** to the company's website by typing the address yourself.
5. **Report the message** as spam or phishing in your email app.
6. **Delete the message.**

> 💡 **Golden Rule:** A real bank, school, or company will **never** ask for your password via email or SMS.

---

#### Habit 5: Regularly Reviewing Your Account Activity 🔍

This means checking your accounts regularly to spot anything unusual.

**What to look for:**
- Logins from cities or countries you have never visited
- Emails or messages you did not send
- Purchases or transactions you did not make
- Password changes you did not request

**What to do if you spot something suspicious:**

1. **Change your password immediately.**
2. **Enable 2FA** if you haven't already.
3. **Log out of all devices** (most platforms have a "Log out of all sessions" option in settings).
4. **Report the suspicious activity** to the platform's support team.

---

#### Habit 6: Backing Up Important Data 💾

**Backing up** means saving a copy of your important files in a second, safe location.

Think about it this way: you write a 10-page project. Your laptop breaks. Without a backup, your project is gone forever. With a backup, you simply open the second copy.

**Where to Back Up Your Data:**

| Method | What It Is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| External Hard Drive | A physical device you plug into your computer | Store a copy of your files on a USB drive or portable hard disk |
| Cloud Storage | Online storage service | Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive |

**How Often Should You Back Up?**
- School projects: after every major editing session
- Important documents: at least once a week
- Photos: at least once a month

> 📌 **Quick Recap:** Responsible computer usage means using strong passwords, enabling 2FA, avoiding public Wi-Fi for sensitive tasks, recognizing phishing scams, checking your account activity, and backing up your data regularly.

## 8.3 Best Practices in Online Behavior

### 🎬 Story Mode: The Post That Could Not Be Deleted

Usman deleted the post. But by then, screenshots existed everywhere. It could never truly be gone.

**The lesson?** Online, your words travel faster than you think — and they can stay forever, even after you delete them. Responsible online behavior means thinking before you act.

### Using Digital Platforms Responsibly

You use digital platforms every day. Let's look at the most common ones and the rules for using each safely.

---

#### 📘 Social Media (Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok)

Social media connects you with friends and the world. But it also exposes you to risks if used carelessly.

**Rules for Responsible Social Media Use:**

- **Never share personal information publicly.** This includes: your home address, school name, daily schedule, phone number, or photos of your location.
- **Think before you post.** Ask: *"Would I be okay if my parents, my teacher, or a stranger saw this?"* If no — don't post it.
- **Check your privacy settings.** Set your account to "Private" so only approved followers can see your posts.
- **Don't accept friend requests from strangers.** You don't know who they really are.
- **Report and block** accounts that make you feel uncomfortable.

> **Practical Walkthrough: Setting Your Instagram Account to Private**
> 1. Open the Instagram app and tap your **profile icon** (bottom right).
> 2. Tap the **three lines** (menu) at the top right.
> 3. Tap **Settings and Privacy**.
> 4. Tap **Account Privacy**.
> 5. Toggle on **Private Account**.
> 6. Tap **Switch to Private** to confirm.
> ✅ Now only people you approve can see your posts.

---

#### 📧 Email

Email is used for school, job applications, and formal communication. It is powerful — and risky if misused.

**Rules for Responsible Email Use:**

- **Never open emails from unknown senders.** Especially if they have attachments or links.
- **Never click links** inside suspicious emails. Instead, go directly to the website by typing its address.
- **Never share your password** via email — not even with someone claiming to be your school's IT department.
- **Use a professional tone** when emailing teachers or officials. Write clearly and politely.
- **Check the sender's email address** before replying.

---

#### ☁️ Cloud Services (Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive)

Cloud services let you store and share files online. They are incredibly useful for school projects and group work.

**Rules for Safe Cloud Service Use:**

- **Use strong passwords** for your cloud accounts.
- **Never upload sensitive documents** (like national ID scans, financial records) unless absolutely necessary.
- **Share files carefully.** When sharing a document, choose "View only" unless you want others to edit it.
- **Log out** of cloud services on shared or public computers.
- **Never store your passwords in a cloud file.** If your account is hacked, all passwords are exposed.

---

#### 📱 Online Applications (Games, Shopping, Learning Apps)

Online apps are fun and useful. But not all apps are safe.

**Rules for Safe App Use:**

- **Download apps only from official stores:** Google Play Store or Apple App Store.
- **Read app permissions carefully.** If a flashlight app asks to access your contacts and camera — that is suspicious.
- **Don't make in-app purchases** without a parent's permission.
- **Read reviews** before downloading an unknown app.
- **Uninstall apps** you no longer use.

---

### Privacy Settings and Data Security

**Privacy settings** are controls inside apps and websites that let you decide who can see your information, who can contact you, and what data is collected about you.

> **Visual Tip — The Privacy Control Panel:**
> Imagine a control panel with switches and sliders. Each switch controls one part of your privacy:
> - Switch 1: Who can see my profile? *(Public / Friends only / Only me)*
> - Switch 2: Who can send me messages? *(Everyone / Friends only / No one)*
> - Switch 3: Who can see my location? *(On / Off)*
> - Switch 4: Who can tag me in photos? *(Everyone / Friends / No one)*
>
> Your job is to set each switch to the safest position that still lets you enjoy the platform.

**Data Security** means protecting the data that platforms collect about you.

Every app and website collects some data about you — your name, location, browsing habits, clicks. This is called **data collection**. Sometimes companies use this data to show you advertisements. Sometimes they share it with other companies.

**How to Protect Your Data:**

1. Read the **Privacy Policy** of any app before signing up (at least the summary).
2. Choose apps from companies that have a clear, understandable privacy policy.
3. Regularly **review which apps have access** to your phone's camera, microphone, and location. Remove access you don't need.
4. Use a **different email address** for non-important apps, so your main email stays clean.

---

> 📌 **Quick Recap:** Best practices in online behavior mean using every digital platform thoughtfully — protecting your information, respecting others, and understanding who can see what you post or share.

## 8.4 Intellectual Property Rights

### 🎬 Story Mode: The Invisible Thief

Bilal spent four months drawing a comic book. He posted the first page on his school's online gallery. Three weeks later, his friend showed him a website selling printed copies of his comic — with someone else's name on it.

Bilal's work had been stolen. But not his physical drawing. The thief had stolen something invisible — his **idea**, his **creation**, his **intellectual work**.

This is called **intellectual property theft** — and it is just as serious as stealing a physical object.

---

### What Is Intellectual Property?

**Intellectual property (IP)** is any creation of the mind. This includes:
- Books, articles, and stories
- Music and songs
- Software and apps
- Paintings, drawings, and photographs
- Inventions and new processes
- Company logos and brand names

**Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)** are the legal rules that protect these creations. They give creators control over how their work is used by others.

There are three main types: **Copyright**, **Trademark**, and **Patent.**

---

### Copyright ©

**Copyright** is a legal right that gives a creator full control over their original work.

When you write a story, draw a picture, compose a song, or write a computer program — **you automatically own the copyright.** No one can copy, share, sell, or modify your work without your permission.

**What Does Copyright Protect?**
- Books and written content
- Music and audio recordings
- Movies and videos
- Photographs
- Software code
- Paintings and drawings

**How Long Does Copyright Last?**
In most countries, copyright lasts for the **lifetime of the creator plus 70 years.** After that, the work becomes "public domain" — free for anyone to use.

**Example:**
When an author writes a novel, she owns the copyright. If a student copies chapters of that novel and distributes them in class without permission — that is a **copyright violation**, even if no money is involved.

**What About Digital Content?**
The same rules apply online. You cannot:
- Copy and paste someone's article into your own blog without credit.
- Download and share a movie without the creator's permission.
- Upload a song to a video without a music license.

> 💡 **Copyright Symbol:** ©. When you see this symbol next to a work, it means the creator has legal protection over it.

---

### Trademark ™ / ®

A **trademark** is a symbol, word, phrase, logo, or design that identifies and distinguishes a company's products or services from others.

**Examples of Famous Trademarks:**
- The Nike "swoosh" ✔
- The McDonald's golden arches M
- The Apple logo 🍎
- Coca-Cola's unique font and bottle shape

**Why Do Trademarks Matter?**
They prevent other companies from using similar logos or names to confuse customers. If you started a shoe company and used the Nike swoosh — you would be violating Nike's trademark.

**Symbols:**
- **™** = The company claims the trademark but it may not yet be legally registered.
- **®** = The trademark is officially registered and has full legal protection.

---

### Patent 🔧

A **patent** protects a **new invention or process.** It gives the inventor the **exclusive right** to make, use, or sell the invention for a certain period of time (usually **20 years**).

**Examples:**
- Alexander Graham Bell patented the first telephone.
- A company that invents a new type of battery can patent it — no other company can copy that battery design for 20 years without permission.

**Why Patents Matter:**
They reward inventors for their hard work. If anyone could copy your invention the moment you released it, nobody would bother inventing new things.

> **Visual Tip — The IP Triangle:**
> Draw a triangle. Label the three corners:
> - **Copyright** (creative works — books, music, software)
> - **Trademark** (brand identity — logos, names)
> - **Patent** (inventions — new products, processes)
>
> In the middle, write: **Intellectual Property Rights**
> This triangle covers everything that "belongs" to a creator's mind.

---

### Ethical and Legal Responsibilities Regarding Intellectual Property Rights

Knowing that IP rights exist is not enough. You must also **behave ethically** — meaning doing the right thing, even when no one is watching.

#### Software Piracy

**Software piracy** means using, copying, or distributing software without buying a legal license.

**Examples of Software Piracy:**
- Downloading a paid app from an unofficial website for free
- Copying a licensed program from one computer to 10 computers using one license
- Using a "cracked" version of software (a version where the payment system has been hacked)

**Why Is Piracy Harmful?**
- It is **illegal.** You can face fines or legal action.
- It **harms creators.** Software developers work hard to create programs. Piracy means they don't get paid for their work.
- Pirated software often contains **malware** — harmful programs hidden inside the fake free software.

#### Plagiarism

**Plagiarism** means using someone else's words or ideas as if they were your own — without giving them credit.

In school: copying from a classmate's essay.
Online: copying text from a website into your report without citing the source.

Plagiarism is:
- **Ethically wrong** — you're taking credit for someone else's hard work.
- **Academically harmful** — teachers can detect it, and consequences are serious.
- **Legally risky** — if the copied work is copyrighted, plagiarism can be a legal violation.

**How to Avoid Plagiarism:**
1. Always write ideas in **your own words.**
2. If you use someone else's words, use **quotation marks** and name the source.
3. At the end of your report, include a **list of sources** you used.

---

### Legal Compliance in Computing

**Legal compliance** in computing means following the laws related to computers, software, data, and the internet.

Key laws that affect computer users:

| Legal Concept | What It Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Copyright Law | You cannot copy or share creative works without permission | Don't copy-paste an article into your own blog without credit |
| Software License Agreement | When you install software, you agree to use it only as specified | One license = one computer, unless the license says otherwise |
| Data Privacy Laws | Organizations must protect the personal data of users | A school cannot share students' personal information without permission |
| Cybercrime Laws | Hacking, online fraud, and cyberbullying are illegal | Sending fake messages to steal someone's password is a crime |

> 💡 **In Pakistan:** The **Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (PECA) 2016** is the main cybercrime law. It covers online harassment, hacking, identity theft, and spreading false information digitally.

---

> 📌 **Quick Recap:** Intellectual property rights protect creative works (copyright), brand identities (trademark), and inventions (patent). Using others' work without permission is both unethical and illegal.

## Social Networking Safety and Online Interactions

### Privacy Settings, Responsible Sharing, and Online Etiquette

### 🎬 Story Mode: The Accidental Overshare

Hira was excited. She had just been accepted into a school competition. She posted on Facebook: *"So happy!!! Going to Lahore on Saturday for the Science Olympiad at [School Name]. Leaving at 8AM, back by 6PM! House will be empty lol 😂"*

She thought it was harmless. But without realizing it, she had just told the entire internet: when she would be away, where she lived (her school district was public on her profile), and that her house would be empty.

Hira meant no harm. But **oversharing** — sharing too much personal information online — can have serious consequences.

---

### Privacy Settings: Your First Line of Defense

Every social media platform has **privacy settings** — controls that let you decide who sees your content.

**Why Are Privacy Settings Important?**
By default, many platforms set your account to **Public**, meaning strangers can see everything you post. Changing your settings to **Private** means only approved followers/friends can see your content.

**Key Privacy Settings to Check on Any Platform:**

| Setting | What to Set It To |
|---|---|
| Who can see my posts? | Friends / Followers only |
| Who can send me messages? | Friends only |
| Who can see my friend/follower list? | Only me / Friends only |
| Who can find me by phone number or email? | Nobody |
| Location sharing | OFF (unless needed for a specific app like Maps) |
| Who can tag me in photos? | Only me to approve tags |

---

### Responsible Sharing

**Responsible sharing** means thinking carefully before you post, share, or send anything online.

**Before you post, ask yourself these three questions:**

1. **Is it true?** Don't share news, rumors, or information you haven't verified.
2. **Is it kind?** Would this hurt or embarrass someone?
3. **Is it necessary?** Does this need to be online? Is sharing this safe?

If the answer to any of these is "No" or "I'm not sure" — **don't post it.**

**What NOT to Share Online:**
- Your home address or daily routine
- Photos that show your location (street signs, school name, landmarks)
- Personal documents (ID cards, school cards, passport)
- Angry or hateful comments about people
- Other people's private photos or messages without their permission

---

### Online Etiquette

**Online etiquette** (also called **netiquette**) is the set of rules for respectful, polite behavior online.

Think of it this way: you have manners in real life — you say please and thank you, you don't shout at people, you don't interrupt. **Online etiquette is the same thing, applied to the digital world.**

**Core Rules of Online Etiquette:**

- **Use respectful language.** Even when you disagree, stay polite.
- **Don't type in ALL CAPS.** Online, all caps means you are SHOUTING.
- **Think before you comment.** Ask: "Would I say this to this person's face in school?"
- **Don't spread rumors or unverified information.**
- **Give credit.** If you share someone's photo or art, name them as the creator.
- **Be patient.** Not everyone replies instantly.
- **Don't spam.** Sending repeated messages is annoying and disrespectful.

> 💡 **The Golden Rule of the Internet:** Treat others online exactly as you would like to be treated offline.

## Addressing Cyberbullying, Harassment, and Respectful Online Interaction

### 🎬 Story Mode: The Anonymous Bully

Omar thought being anonymous online meant he could say anything. He created a fake account and spent weeks leaving cruel comments on a classmate's social media posts. He thought it was funny. His classmate, Zara, started skipping school. She stopped sleeping well. She cried every day.

One day, the school's cybercrime awareness officer traced the account. Omar was identified. His parents were called. He faced serious disciplinary action.

Omar learned a painful lesson: **There is no true anonymity online. And cruelty — whether online or offline — has real consequences for real people.**

---

### What Is Cyberbullying?

**Cyberbullying** is when someone uses digital technology — phones, social media, online games, messaging apps — to repeatedly harass, threaten, embarrass, or harm another person.

**Forms of Cyberbullying:**

| Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Harassment | Sending mean, hurtful messages repeatedly | Constant cruel DMs targeting one person |
| Exclusion | Deliberately leaving someone out of online groups | Removing a person from a WhatsApp group publicly |
| Impersonation | Creating a fake account pretending to be someone | Making a fake Instagram account with someone's photos |
| Cyberstalking | Tracking and monitoring someone online without consent | Following someone's every post and commenting threats |
| Outing | Sharing someone's private information or photos publicly | Posting someone's private messages to embarrass them |

---

### What to Do If You Are Being Cyberbullied

**Step 1: Don't Respond.**
Responding often makes things worse. The bully wants a reaction. Don't give them one.

**Step 2: Document the Evidence.**
Take screenshots of all bullying messages, comments, or posts. Save them. You will need them when reporting.

**Step 3: Block the Bully.**
On every platform:
- Instagram: Go to the person's profile → tap the three dots (⋮) → tap **Block**.
- Facebook: Go to their profile → click the three dots → click **Block**.
- WhatsApp: Open the chat → tap the three dots → **More** → **Block**.

**Step 4: Report the Bullying to the Platform.**
All major platforms have reporting tools. Use them. The platform can investigate and remove the bully's account.

**Step 5: Tell a Trusted Adult.**
Tell a parent, teacher, or school counselor. You do not have to face this alone. In Pakistan, cyberbullying can be reported to the **FIA Cybercrime Wing** (Federal Investigation Agency).

**Step 6: Take Care of Yourself.**
Being bullied online is painful. Talk to someone you trust. Remember: the bully's words say everything about them and nothing about your worth.

---

### What to Do If You SEE Cyberbullying Happening to Someone Else

You are not powerless. You are a bystander — and bystanders have the power to stop bullying.

**Your actions as a bystander:**

1. **Do NOT share, like, or comment** on bullying content. Doing so amplifies the harm.
2. **Reach out to the person being bullied** privately. A simple message — *"I see what's happening. That's not okay. I'm here for you"* — can make a huge difference.
3. **Report the content** to the platform using the report button.
4. **Tell a trusted adult** — a teacher, a parent, or a school counselor.

> 💡 **Remember:** Staying silent when you see bullying is a choice — and it is a choice to let it continue. You have the power to be part of the solution.

---

### Respectful Online Interaction: Building Positive Digital Communities

Being a responsible digital citizen is not just about avoiding bad behavior. It is about **actively creating a positive online environment.**

**How to be a positive force online:**

- **Lift others up.** Leave encouraging comments on people's work.
- **Share knowledge generously.** If you know something helpful, share it — with credit.
- **Correct mistakes kindly.** If someone shares wrong information, correct them politely — not with mockery.
- **Celebrate diversity.** People from different backgrounds, cultures, and viewpoints make the internet richer. Embrace that.
- **Protect others' privacy.** Never share someone else's personal information or photos without their clear permission.

---

> 📌 **Quick Recap:** Online safety means setting strong privacy controls, sharing only what is necessary and appropriate, treating others with respect, responding to cyberbullying firmly and safely, and actively helping to build positive digital communities.
